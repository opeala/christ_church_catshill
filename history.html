Our Roots & Legacy

History & Heritage

Discover the fascinating past of Christ Church Catshill, from its 1838 foundation to its literary ties with A.E. Housman.

A Victorian Foundation

While our congregation is firmly focused on serving the village today, our Victorian building carries a rich and unique heritage. Explore the significant milestones that shaped Christ Church over nearly two centuries.

1838

Construction of Christ Church

Christ Church was formally built and consecrated, establishing a permanent place of worship right at the heart of the growing Catshill community.

First Vicar appointed: Reverend Thomas Housman
Easter 1859

The Housman Baptism

The world-famous author and poet A.E. Housman was baptised here on Easter Day by his grandfather, the church's very first Vicar, Reverend Thomas Housman.

Visitors can still see the miniature font in the church, which is reputedly the exact vessel used by Thomas Housman to baptise the infant poet.

Heritage Grounds

The Historic Churchyard

The grounds of Christ Church hold deep ties to the Housman family and local history:

  • Thomas Housman's Grave: Located in the far corner of the churchyard, easily recognizable by its striking fourteen-foot stone cross.
  • The Housman Trees: As you enter the churchyard, you will see two horse chestnut trees—surviving specimens of the original six planted by Edward Housman (father of A.E. Housman), with one tree planted for each of his six children.
  • God's Acre: A traditional cherry tree is always maintained in the churchyard to commemorate Housman's famous poem of the same name.

Plan Your Visit

Come and experience our historic building in person. Whether you are tracing your literary roots or looking for a quiet space for prayer, you are always welcome.
